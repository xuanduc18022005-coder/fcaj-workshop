## Internship experience

First Cloud AI Journey allowed me to learn AWS from fundamentals to practice. Initially, the number of unfamiliar services made it difficult to understand how infrastructure, access control and applications fit together. Community talks and self-study helped me develop a habit of reading official documentation, testing one step at a time and recording issues.

Looking back on the 12-week internship, I consider First Cloud AI Journey valuable for students entering cloud computing. The combination of foundational knowledge, guided self-study, technical talks and team projects helped me understand how AWS services work together to solve a concrete problem.

IAM, VPC, serverless and CloudWatch supported NetMon directly. AI agent governance and incident investigation broadened my understanding of operations and evidence-based decisions. Teamwork reinforced the importance of API contracts, task allocation, issue tracking and testing components before integration.

I appreciate the opportunity to learn with the community and explore new technologies. My main challenges were the breadth of documentation, English technical terms and AWS configuration issues. I hope the program retains its hands-on focus and provides additional guidance for beginners.

Overall, the internship clarified my next learning priorities: networking fundamentals, serverless development, application security and testing. These experiences provide a foundation for more independent work on future projects.

## Advice for new learners

- Understand IAM, MFA, Regions and AWS Budgets before creating resources; never share personal access keys.
- Learn through small labs. Inspect logs and isolate faults rather than changing many settings at once.
- Use Git, agree on API contracts and responsibilities, and preserve stable versions before integration.
- Maintain a worklog and capture evidence at the right time; distinguish demos from live AWS results.

## Suggestions for the program

| Area | Suggestion |
|---|---|
| Learning material | Share objectives, prerequisites and short labs before meetups. |
| Technical support | Offer Q&A on IAM, CORS, JWT and costs, with self-checklists for labs. |
| Team projects | Schedule a mid-project review of architecture, scope, testing and responsibilities. |
| Community events | Allow more discussion time and share materials or a recap after events. |
| Internship reporting | Provide a consistent template and assessment criteria so students can review their evidence before submission. |

These suggestions reflect my learning experience and aim to make future cohorts more accessible, especially to first-time AWS learners.
